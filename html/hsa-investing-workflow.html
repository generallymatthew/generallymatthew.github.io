Concept

HSA Investing

An HSA that invests the money above your cash cushion, and refills the cushion when it runs low, without asking you to manage it.

Overview

A Health Savings Account is the most tax-advantaged account most people will ever hold (contributions, growth, and qualified withdrawals are all untaxed), yet most of the money in these accounts sits in cash. The reason isn't apathy. It's that "invest your HSA" tools ask the account holder to do a job they never signed up for: predict this year's medical spending, keep enough liquid to cover it, move the rest into funds, and unwind that position every time a claim hits.

This concept removes that job. During a one-time setup the member draws a single line: the cash cushion they want to keep spendable. From then on the account runs itself: money above the line is swept into the member's chosen funds, and anything that pulls the balance below the line is sold back to restore it, following rules the member set once. The interface interrupts for exactly one event: spending more than the cushion holds.

The problem

Two hard things are tangled together. Investing well is one problem; forecasting irregular medical costs is another. Existing HSA investing features hand the member both at once and offer a brokerage screen as the answer. So the safe move is to leave everything in cash, and years of tax-free compounding are quietly lost to inflation.

The insight

There is only one number the member actually has an opinion about: how much cash they want within reach. Every other decision (when to invest, how much, what to sell when a bill lands) can be derived from that number plus a few rules captured up front. Design the account around the cushion line and the rest becomes bookkeeping the system can do silently.

The approach

The flow is built as one setup and three engines:

  • Setup is strictly linear: seven short steps, a progress rail that never disappears, and nothing goes live until a final review screen. The cash target is pre-seeded from the member's own claim history, so the default is already close to right.
  • Invest the surplus runs on every contribution. Cash above the line is invested at the member's mix; they see a receipt, never a prompt.
  • Replenish the cushion runs after a claim or a market dip drops cash below the line. The account sells per the member's sell order and states plainly what happened and why.
  • Spend past the cushion is the one flow that stops the member. A payment larger than spendable cash opens a single screen with three ways out (sell just the gap, raise the target, or cancel), with the gentlest option preselected.
Principles that run through every screen
  • Set decisions once. Anything that can be a standing rule is captured at setup, not re-asked later.
  • Automate the routine; interrupt only for the exceptional. Sweeps and replenishments are logged, not confirmed.
  • Say what the system did, and why. Every automatic sale names its cause, whether a claim or a price move, in plain language.
  • The cushion line is always on screen. The member can see where they stand against their target from any account view.

What I'd validate next

  • Trust in silent selling. Will members accept an account that sells positions without asking first? The honesty of the replenish notice is doing a lot of work and needs testing against real reactions.
  • The seeded cash target. Does a target derived from claim history land close enough that members accept the default, or does it feel arbitrary and get overridden every time?
  • The one interruption. Does the pre-spend screen read as the system protecting the member, or as being blocked from their own money?
  • Setup length. Seven steps is a lot. Which steps can be defaulted away entirely for members who choose "managed for me"?
